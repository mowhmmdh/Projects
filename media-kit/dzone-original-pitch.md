# DZone Original Article Pitch

## Proposed title
Operational Network Hardening: A Practical Lifecycle for IT Teams

## Summary
Network hardening is frequently reduced to firewall rules and exposed-port checks. In real IT environments, security depends on a broader set of controls and operational habits. This proposed article presents a practical lifecycle for hardening enterprise networks: establish a baseline, map trust boundaries, segment traffic, protect administrative access, harden core services and servers, monitor behavior, validate controls, document changes, and repeat the cycle as the environment changes.

The article is vendor-neutral and aimed at practitioners. It uses generalized real-world IT operations examples while avoiding confidential architecture, internal addressing, credentials, proprietary configurations, or customer information.

## Proposed structure
1. Why firewall-only hardening fails
2. Establishing a useful infrastructure baseline
3. Designing segmentation around actual communication needs
4. Protecting the management plane
5. Hardening identity, DNS, Windows, and Linux infrastructure
6. Monitoring, validation, and evidence
7. Change control, rollback, and keeping the hardened state intact
8. A practical repeatable checklist

## Audience
Network engineers, infrastructure engineers, system administrators, DevOps/IT operations practitioners, and security professionals.
