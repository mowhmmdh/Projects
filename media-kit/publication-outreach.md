# Publication Outreach — Independent Coverage Roadmap

## Priority order

### 1. DZone
**Goal:** Publish the English network-hardening article as an original technical contribution.
**Route:** Create/sign in to a DZone account → Post → Post an Article → Send to Moderation.
**Status:** Article and pitch prepared.
**Editorial fit:** Strong for practical infrastructure/security engineering content.

### 2. InfoQ
**Goal:** Submit a practitioner-driven proposal based on real production lessons.
**Route:** Submit the proposal through InfoQ's article submission process.
**Status:** Proposal prepared.
**Editorial fit:** Strong topic fit, but substantially more selective; InfoQ currently states that only about 10% of article submissions are published and limits authors to one proposal per quarter.

### 3. Opensource.com
**Goal:** Submit a practical, vendor-neutral technical article with an open-source/Linux angle.
**Route:** Email the article idea to open@opensource.com.
**Status:** Pitch prepared.
**Editorial fit:** Good when the article has a clear open-source/Linux connection.

## Evidence rules

- Do not pay for fake coverage or guaranteed Wikipedia links.
- Do not create articles that impersonate independent journalism.
- Do not claim publication until a third-party publisher actually publishes the work.
- Keep bylines, publication dates, article URLs, and editorial correspondence archived.
- Distinguish self-published portfolio material from independent secondary coverage.

## Wikipedia milestone

Target article creation only after there is meaningful coverage in multiple independent, reliable secondary sources. Self-published portfolio pages, GitHub repositories, LinkedIn profiles, and personal social posts are useful evidence of the underlying work but do not substitute for independent coverage for a notability case.
