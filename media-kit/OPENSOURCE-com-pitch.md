# Opensource.com Pitch — Network Hardening Beyond the Firewall

**To:** open@opensource.com

**Subject:** Article idea: A practical, layered approach to network hardening

Hello Opensource.com editorial team,

I’d like to propose an original technical article for the Opensource.com community:

**A Practical Approach to Network Hardening Beyond the Firewall**

The article explains how infrastructure teams can approach hardening as a layered operational process rather than treating the firewall as the main security control. It covers asset and service baselining, least privilege, management-path restrictions, network segmentation, unnecessary-service reduction, patching, independent backups, logging, and post-change verification.

The angle is practical and vendor-neutral, with an emphasis on lessons that can be applied to Linux and mixed enterprise environments. The article is based on hands-on IT and infrastructure work and will not include confidential employer information, credentials, internal addressing, or proprietary configurations.

Proposed takeaways:

1. Build a baseline before changing production infrastructure.
2. Reduce administrative privilege and management exposure.
3. Use segmentation to limit the impact of incidents.
4. Treat patching, backups, and logging as security controls.
5. Validate every hardening change instead of assuming the configuration is correct.

The article would be approximately 1,800–2,500 words and written as an educational technical guide, without promotional content.
